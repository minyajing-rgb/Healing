# Evidence, Safety & Cultural Rights Policy / 证据、安全与文化权利标准

## 1. Why this exists

Earth Healing includes biomedical, historical, cultural, subjective, spiritual and metaphysical materials. These cannot be merged into one truth label.

The repository preserves traditions **and** makes epistemic status visible.

---

## 2. Claim separation

Each claim must declare one or more types:

- historical_record
- traditional_use
- living_practice
- cultural_symbolism
- subjective_wellbeing
- mechanistic_hypothesis
- preclinical
- clinical_claim
- spiritual_metaphysical
- commercial_claim

Example:

“某植物 historically used for sleep” is not the same record as  
“某植物 clinically improves insomnia.”

Store separately.

---

## 3. Evidence attaches to claims

Never assign a blanket “E4” to an entire plant, tradition or therapy.

A plant may have:
- E4 evidence for one standardized preparation/indication,
- E1 for another constituent,
- E0 for a spiritual use,
- and a separate T3 traditional record.

---

## 4. Sound / chant / frequency

Mandatory separation:

- measured acoustics;
- music/voice participation;
- breathing/social effects of chanting;
- vibroacoustic delivery;
- binaural/entrainment products;
- spiritual/metaphysical “frequency/resonance” claims.

If a tradition says a sound “clears energy,” record that as a **traditional/spiritual claim** unless a separate modern outcome has been studied.

---

## 5. Herbs, essential oils and apothecary preparations

Each ingestible/topical/inhaled material should track:

- exact botanical identity where possible
- part used
- preparation
- concentration
- route
- dose only when sourced and safe to publish
- adulteration/contamination risk
- allergy
- drug interaction
- pregnancy/lactation
- pediatric risk
- liver/kidney considerations where documented
- photosensitivity where relevant
- toxic constituents
- regulatory status
- evidence by indication

Essential oils must not be treated as harmless merely because they are “natural.”

---

## 6. Needling, heat and manual procedures

Track:
- practitioner qualification/licensing
- sterility/infection controls
- anatomical risk
- burn risk
- bleeding risk
- contraindications
- adverse events
- country regulation

Instructional content must distinguish cultural/history education from professional clinical training.

---

## 7. Ritual, folk, shamanic and Indigenous knowledge

Rules:
1. Attribute to a specific community/tradition where possible.
2. Do not publish sacred/private operational details merely because a secondary source mentions them.
3. Use `cultural_access` status.
4. Avoid decontextualized “DIY ritual recipes.”
5. Do not label unrelated traditions “shamanic” or “witchcraft.”
6. Record community ownership/attribution notes where known.
7. Never expose sensitive sacred coordinates on public maps.

---

## 8. Animals

Two distinct branches:

### A. Human–animal interaction
Animal-assisted interventions, companion animals, equine programs etc.

Track:
- participant safety
- allergy/infection considerations
- animal welfare
- practitioner/program standards

### B. Animal-derived materia medica
Track:
- species
- conservation status
- legal trade status
- welfare/ethical issues
- substitutes where documented
- public-access restrictions

No endangered-species exploitation instructions.

---

## 9. Plants & conservation

Track:
- accepted scientific name
- synonyms
- native/cultivated distribution
- conservation status
- harvest pressure
- sustainable cultivation
- protected/trade restrictions when relevant

Map origin, biological distribution and cultural use separately.

---

## 10. Red flags

Public-facing content should direct users toward licensed care/emergency services rather than self-treatment when discussing:
- emergency symptoms;
- poisoning;
- severe allergic reaction;
- serious infection;
- acute psychiatric crisis;
- dangerous pregnancy complications;
- major trauma;
- uncontrolled bleeding;
- other time-critical conditions.

---

## 11. Source hierarchy

Preferred evidence/source families:

### Primary / authoritative
- historical primary texts and critical editions
- community/heritage institutions
- official pharmacopoeias
- government/health regulators
- WHO and national health agencies
- peer-reviewed systematic reviews and trials
- botanical taxonomic authorities
- conservation authorities

### Secondary
- university/museum collections
- reputable monographs
- ethnographies
- academic reference works

### Discovery only
- blogs
- social media
- commercial wellness pages
- unsourced videos
- product marketing

Discovery-only sources cannot independently promote a record to REVIEWED.

---

## 12. Publication states

`PLANNED` — idea exists  
`SOURCE_FOUND` — at least one source located  
`EXTRACTED` — claims extracted  
`STRUCTURED` — schema-compliant  
`REVIEWED` — source/evidence/safety/cultural QA  
`BILINGUAL` — zh/en verified  
`MAP_READY` — location relationships verified  
`TIMELINE_READY` — dating fields verified  
`PUBLISHED` — front-end eligible

No “complete” label should be used for a category merely because the folder exists.
